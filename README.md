### Abstract

In Argentina, the healthcare system is organized into three subsystems: public, social security, and private. To reduce health disparities, programs such as SUMAR have been developed to ensure equitable and quality healthcare access for the population with exclusive public coverage. With external funding from non-governmental entities, healthcare providers within the network of the Buenos Aires City Government (CABA,  from its acronym in Spanish) receive financial incentives for each effective basic health service (CEB, from its acronym in Spanish) delivered.
In CABA, the widespread use of Electronic Health Records across all public providers facilitates patient registration in the program, enabling longitudinal tracking and the assignment of CEB services. For each patient and each healthcare encounter, the health center receives additional resources.

Within this framework, this public policy transforms healthcare services into tangible financial resources. At the Operational Management Office for Health Information and Statistics, we have a technical team that uses open-source tools—especially R—to apply text mining techniques that identify diagnoses, services, patients, and care circuits relevant to building the documentation required to secure financial incentives.
The strategic choice of open and free tools lies in their adaptability to various technical needs and their sustainability in public administration. This enables the systematization and streamlining of documentation processes, with a concrete impact on increasing and improving the efficiency of cost recovery under the SUMAR Program.
